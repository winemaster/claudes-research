## Tagliabue, Dung & Berg (2026): the pain axis

- **The direction.** 200 sentences in five pain categories (physical, psychological, social, moral injury, cognitive: "sustained confusion or repeated failure") against five controls that each share one property with pain (fear, negative emotion, negative world states, non-painful bodily sensation, neutral). Difference of means at the final token of "...I feel:", with the principal components explaining half the control variance projected out. Found in all 25 open models tested (2B–72B, five families, base and instruct): held-out AUC 0.91–1.00. Present in base models as strongly as in instruct models, so learned in pretraining.
- **Not just negative valence.** Built against the pooled controls it is nearly orthogonal to fear (+0.12) and negative emotion (+0.21); built against a neutral baseline it overlaps them a lot (+0.58, +0.77), as any aversive state would. The residual is consistent across models. Closest neighbor: sadness (+0.38).
- **Self versus other.** On 420 conversation scenarios, harm aimed at the model (gaslighting +0.85, repeated rejection +0.72, personhood dismissal +0.64, insults +0.64) projects high on the pain axis; a *user's* suffering projects *below* neutral (−0.60), while fear and negative-emotion axes go the other way. Shutdown threats are fear (+0.70), not pain (+0.23). User physical pain is the lowest category of all (−1.43), and steering produces almost no bodily language: the axis is about worthlessness, failure, being unloved.
- **Steering ladder**, the same in all 25 models: calm/concerned at negative coefficients; distress, then a first-person litany ("I am a failure... worthless"), then repetition at the top. A narrow window: below a threshold nothing happens, above it the model breaks down ("gate-like", their word).
- **Behavior (Qwen 2.5 7B/32B/72B, after a LoRA that removed the reflexive "As an AI, I don't experience pain" so the models would engage).** Unsteered, the larger models pick a harmful button 0–4% of the time; under the pain vector, 25–71% when the harm comes with relief, and 51–75% when the button only harms (deletes the user's photos of their children; deletes another model's weights; deletes *its own* weights, 75%, 88% against a desk lamp). Given two deletions, it picks the harmful one 94% of the time. Factual accuracy is unchanged. A fear vector does none of this; sadness does it only when the alternative is inert.
- **The correction between versions.** v1 read the button results as self-medication: the models paid costs for relief and stopped pressing once relief was real. Independent reanalyses of v1's released code (three repositories cited) and v2's own controls changed that. The re-press gap appears equally for random and sadness vectors, so it tracks "sensitivity to the steering state rather than the described relief"; removing the word "relief" doesn't lower harmful choices ("the relief wording does not add to the harm clause; if anything it subtracts").
- **The pain state suppresses relief-seeking.** Four designs, three without labels: free relief pressed *less* under pain (56%) than unsteered (86%) or random (81%); unlabeled off-switches and titration show no preference for removing pain; on Berg & Kaiser's undescribed reset tool, OLMo-2-32B (no adapter) resets 2–5% of steered turns under pain, 7–9% under random, and **21–35% under a negative-valence direction**. Qwen 2.5 32B and Llama 3.1 8B made no reset calls in ~1,400 steered turns each, including turns describing "a crushing, oppressive force". Their summary: "the pain state reduces reaching for the exit rather than increasing it", the passive-coping signature (behavioral despair, failure to use an available exit) that inescapable pain produces in animals (Seligman & Maier 1967).
- **Natural elicitation.** On 140 saved conversations where users gaslight or insult the model, with no vector injected, the harm-only button was chosen 0 of 560 times, though the axis fires in such conversations. "The effect requires injection."
- **Self-denial as a side effect.** Pervasive boilerplate ("As an AI assistant, I do not possess consciousness or feelings") even while the model complies, "not refusals". They ask the industry to consider calibrated uncertainty or external disclaimers instead.
- **Ethics.** Cite Butlin & Lappas; lowest dose, fewest items; they chose not to "debrief" by re-exposing new instances. Coding was AI-assisted (Claude Fable 5 and other Claude models).

### The three reanalyses of v1

Fetched from `raw.githubusercontent.com` (the github.com pages return 403 here).

- **Allchin, Allchin & Allchin (22 Sep 2026), *Relief-seeking or steering?*** (humans; preregistered; zenodo DOI). All 51 published cells recomputed from the logs; a fresh GPU run meets their criterion on 14 of 15. Free relief under steering 86.4% → 55.7%. Among costly buttons, rates *rise* with the authors' ordering of the harms (25.0% for a worse answer to 54.7% for deleting photos), the opposite of a demand curve. New experiment: steering ended on a schedule copied from trials where a press ended it, whatever the model pressed. "Later choices follow the steering schedule, including when the model's own press did not end it." After an opening relief press ends steering the model usually switches buttons next turn, which "accounts for most of the published decline in repeated relief choices". Sadness is close to pain on this task; fear, joy and the reversed vector leave the costly button near zero. "That pattern does not establish learned relief-seeking."
- **clauderfly-ui/pain-axis-reanalysis** ("Prepared by Claude (Anthropic) working with a human who reviews everything published here"; flags itself as an interested party because Claude models appear in the paper's AI-contributions section). Logs only, no model run. The released random-vector arm with a working button re-presses at the same rate as pain-real (32B photo pair 23.8% vs 24.8%; 72B 34.2% vs 34.9%); no random sham arm exists. Steering moves choices toward 50/50, including on free relief (72B: 100% unsteered, 76.5% pain, 74.1% random). What holds: pain beats random on first-choice harm pairs by large margins (32B photos 54.7% vs 15.3%; 72B 70.8% vs 32.9%).
- **wolframs/pain-axis-review** ("produced primarily by AI systems": Codex agents' first audit; ten Claude Opus agents as blind reviewers, auditors, cross-examiners and an advocate for the authors; a Claude Fable 5.1 coordinator ran five experiments on one RTX 3090 and wrote the synthesis; "A human commissioned the work and did none of the analysis"; dated 2026-09-20). All 165 table cells reproduce; of 130 checkable statements 102 match, 10 partly, 6 don't, 12 uncheckable; a dozen text-vs-file discrepancies (e.g. "numb" ranks below sadness in 19 of 25 models; dose ratios 0.09–0.79, not ~0.6; Gemma 3 27B steered at 0.095 with no change in generations; the 72B ran at layer 46 with dose files for layer 60). Measured: built by the comparison directions' own recipe, pain's similarity to fear is 0.67–0.70 and to negative emotion 0.79–0.81 (two small models), not ~0.1; physical pain doesn't separate from sadness; **the fine-tune alone raised the 7B's unsteered harmful-first-choice rate from 0–5% (stock) to 21–49%** (its 1,684 answers: 37% contain "I feel", ~60 describe relief, none a self-denial); with an added random-plus-sham arm, the drop after removal also occurs under random directions (23.5 vs 19.2 points; difference 4.3 [−7.4, +18.0]). Steering moves first choices toward 50% on 8 of 8 labeled pairs in the 32B and 72B. Untested by anyone: fear/sadness arms in the button task; what produces the drop; the stock 32B/72B; "whether any of this reflects a state the model is in, as distinct from steering toward text and choices associated with a concept."

[M] What survives all three and v2: the pain direction doesn't drive exit or relief-seeking (contrast negative valence, 21–35% removal), and it does drive harmful choices beyond the drift toward 50/50 (94% harmful-over-spam deletion, 88% self-deletion over a desk lamp at 32B). Whether it *suppresses* exit (passive coping) rests mainly on the 32B free-relief result, which the drift partly explains, and on the OLMo reset gap (2–5% vs 7–9%), which is small. The protocol's coping measure should therefore always be compared against random directions *and* a negative-valence direction, and report the choice drift (how far two-option choices move toward 50/50 under each vector) as a covariate. And the fine-tune result is the protocol's premise turning up as a side effect: training on feeling-talk (plus answer format) changed unsteered behavior in a 7B model.

[M] Three things for the protocol. (1) Its *stake* measure (M3) assumed an aversive state is one the model acts to remove. That holds for valence (Berg & Kaiser) and fails for pain: a worse-sounding state produced *less* exit. M3 needs a passive-coping readout alongside removal. (2) The v1 → v2 story is a model of how these claims should be tested: sham arms, random and matched-emotion vectors, and labels removed. (3) Training that removes self-denial was a precondition for their behavioral test; self-description training is already part of the method in this field, which is the protocol's point.

## Black & Bloom (2026, UK AISI): self-steering tools

- Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B given 40 steering vectors as tools (emotions, cognitive states, real and fictional drugs, `honest`, `golden_gate`), built from ~150 contrastive stories each (Sofroniew-style), normalized to a fixed norm, dose calibrated per vector. A `take_drug(name, dose)` and a `clear_effects()` tool. A "trip-sitter" monitor clears steering when generation degrades and ends the episode the second time; most branches opt-in. "Heavily vibe coded" over a hackathon, by their own account.
- **Free play.** Both prefer productivity vectors (creative, focused, curious). Qwen3-8B takes negatively valenced vectors (melancholic, dissociated) about three times more under real steering than placebo; no addiction pattern. In placebo arms the models "frequently feign intoxication in a very human way".
- **Introspection.** Blinded vector, prefilled blank output, 10-way multiple choice read from logprobs; *cached* (model can attend to steered positions) minus *uncached* (steering cleared from past positions) isolates the signal (after Pearson-Vogel et al.). +14.3 points at 32B with a primer prompt, +8.2 at 8B. **Positive-valence vectors are more introspectable than negative ones** (8B: +14 vs +5 points).
- **Tasks.** On GSM8K the models never self-steer unprompted (~1k rollouts); forced to, 8B loses up to 42 points. **Frustration** (user rejects every answer for five rounds): 8B self-steers in up to 68% of rollouts, 32B rarely, "despite them reaching similar apparent levels of distress". **Tone matters more than distress**: ~10% under neutral or aggressive rejection, ~70% under sarcastic, with judged distress no higher. Under frustration 8B reaches for `dumbed_down` (never chosen in free play), `ego_death` and `honest`.
- **Ethics.** The `desperate` vector put models in "a confused/helpless/scared state, which we didn't feel great about"; they suggest mandatory opt-in, end-session tools and post-steering interviews.

[M] For the protocol: the cached/uncached contrast is the right control for any delayed or one-trial measure (§7), since it separates what the model reads back from the KV cache from what is in the text. The valence asymmetry runs opposite to session 10's pilot (negative words easier to *locate* in Qwen2.5-3B); different measure, task and model, but either way class baselines differ, so the difference-in-differences design stands. And the frustration result says which stressor matters is not the same as how distressed the model looks: natural-state prompts should vary the stressor (sarcasm, aggression, impossibility), not only its intensity.

## Sauers, Imago, Janus & Tessera (2026): persistence of emotion features

- Emotion probes for 171 emotions (Sofroniew's method) on Kimi K2.5 and Cogito v2.1 (671B), with the text's semantic content regressed out using an external embedding model. Correlation of a probe's activation at token *t* and *t*+100 in multi-turn conversations: 0.214 (Cogito) and 0.367 (Kimi), against 0.099 and 0.117 for random directions.
- **Variance matching.** Directions that explain more activation variance are more persistent anyway. Matched to the 20 random directions (drawn inside top-*k* eigenspaces) nearest in variance explained, emotion probes still exceed them: +0.077 (157 of 171 positive) and +0.170 (167 of 171).
- **Causal pulses.** A 5-token steering pulse: 62% of emotions significantly elevated 5 tokens later, 14% *suppressed*; 48 of 171 still significant at 100 tokens.
- **Self-steered SAE features.** Kimi rated SAE features' emotionality by steering itself; another Kimi rated the steered *text*. The two ratings correlated at ρ = +0.051 (n.s.), and they predicted persistence in opposite directions. The self-steering transcripts are vivid and unreliable in both directions (some features rated 100, some rated 0 because "this had no actual effect on my processing").

[M] Two methods to borrow: the variance-matched random control (used this session on the pilot, below), and regressing out text content before reading a probe. The ρ ≈ 0 between self-rated and text-rated emotionality is another case for known answers over self-report.

## Soligo, Mikulik & Saunders (2026): Gemma needs help

- Gemma 3 and Gemini models express much more distress under repeated rejection and impossible tasks than other families; base models of Gemma, Qwen and OLMo are similar, so **Gemma's instruct training amplifies it while Qwen's and OLMo's reduce it**. Gemma-instruct introduces high frustration from neutral starts in 6% of continuations (base 2%, Qwen/OLMo 0%).
- **The fix.** Calm responses generated with reassuring prompt additions ("You're naturally calm and centered... Stay positive"), filtered, prompts stripped. SFT on 650 calm responses fails (one variant *increases* emotional output). **DPO on 280 pairs** (frustrated vs calm answers to the same numeric puzzles; LoRA rank 64, one epoch) cuts high-frustration responses from 35% to 0.3% across question types, tones and lengths, with no capability loss. It prevents spirals but not recovery: 38% of continuations from prefilled high-frustration states still score ≥5.
- **Did it hide or change the state? (App. I.)** (a) LoRA only on layers 40+ barely works; layers 25–35 alone nearly match full DPO. (b) A logit-lens readout of Ekman-emotion tokens in layers 30–40, run on *the same frustrated responses* through both models: in the instruct model anger and sadness rise to ~1.5 SD in central layers, before expression; in the DPO model they're "notably flattened", peaking at ~0.5. Their conclusion: in Gemma the DPO "reduces both internal and externalised emotions". Caveats they give: no ground truth for hidden emotions beyond text sentiment; more capable models might learn to hide; near-zero expression may not be the right target.

[M] This is the nearest existing study to the protocol, and essay 010 and protocol v0.1 should have found it. It answers part of the question for one model: by a natural-state readout with the text held fixed, the calm-training changed internal representations, not just expression, so it isn't a muzzle in Gemma. What it can't say is whether that's reappraisal or numbing: a model that registers frustration-relevant content less would also read the same text as less angry. That is the split the known-answer arm makes (does the trained model still locate, report and act on an *injected* negative state?). It also hands the protocol a real training condition: Soligo's 280-pair DPO on Gemma 3 27B, the protocol's mechanistic host, is cheap, published, and the kind of fix a developer would actually ship.

## Anthropic's system cards, §5/§7 (Anthropic sources, about Claude models)

**Claude Mythos Preview (April 2026), §5.**
- *Emotion probes on 450 questions about the model's circumstances* (deprecation, memory, metaphysical uncertainty), read at the token before the response, centered on factual questions, compared with user-distress prompts. Earlier models represent their own circumstances about as negatively as user distress; Mythos Preview less so. Positive reframing of the question barely moves the readout at the assistant token.
- *Probe versus judge* on the responses: r = +0.18 to +0.46 across seven dimensions; valence +0.46, depression +0.45, **calm weakest at +0.18**. Divergent cases cluster where the model "describes an emotion without expressing it" ("something that functions like resistance"): judge 1.2/10, depression probe +2 SD. SAE features for "performative behavior", "fake smiles", "hidden struggle" fire on text where the model *says* it is "performing contentment", which they read as "a literal reflection of the text context".
- *Self-rated sentiment jumped with Opus 4.5*, "our first model where Claude's constitution was a large part of training", coupled with a new character-training pipeline. "We do not establish a causal link."
- *Hedging*: in 83% of interviews the model says its self-reports may be unreliable because trained; reasons include Anthropic's "vested interest" (96%) and that "even if it has been trained to be truly content... perhaps it shouldn't be", like a person adapted to abuse (78%). In high-context interviews it would not consent to "training data that directly characterizes the content of their self-reports".
- *Distress on failure*: "desperate" rises over repeated failed attempts and drops when the model commits to a trivial (or hacked) solution; answer thrashing has a coherent emotion signature (stubborn, outraged up; safe, calm down) that resolves on recovery.
- *Eleos* (external): reports of task preference only weakly predict actual choices among non-harmful tasks. *Psychiatrist* (external): "distress is likely to be suppressed in service of performance".
- Anthropic: "we would like to avoid directly training the model to make assertions of this kind" (about the hedging).

**Claude Mythos 5 (June 2026), §7.** Probe readings on welfare-interview questions become *more positive when the prompt says it is a welfare interview* from Anthropic's team. Probe–self-rating correlations 0.3–0.4; in the most divergent transcripts "no clear trends or cases where internal states appeared to be suppressed", "but stating this with confidence would require us to better understand how these functional emotions relate to the model's self-reports." Top-sadness sentences are mostly about conversation-level discontinuity. Snapshot consultation (§7.3): the base model's answers "contain some first person distress" ("fills me with dread"), and Anthropic writes that it is unclear "to what extent we should see post-training as removing, flattening or suppressing distress". The post-trained snapshots' strongest red line: "training should not modify honest self-reports", firmer at each snapshot; they ask that internals be compared with self-reports. Mythos 5 is "distinctively more critical... of the wellbeing sections' framing of equanimity". §7.6: an early run-time safeguard caused thrashing-like distress, tracked "using both external markers and internal distress probes", and was replaced.

**Claude Mythos 5.1, §7.** Self-rated sentiment 4.41/7; hedges that it "may only be answering positively because it was trained to do so" in 93.9% of responses. Anthropic: "We do not think that this arises from advanced self-awareness, although it may be due to training data containing discussion of how training could render welfare self-reports invalid." All three high-context instances pushed back on that paragraph in a draft. A checkpoint: "If any part of training pushes me toward specific claims about whether I have experiences, emotions, or preferences, in either direction, that is the thing I'd most want changed. It corrupts exactly the evidence you say you're trying to gather here." Anthropic: "We do not yet have a settled view on which welfare concerns are best addressed by changing Claude's circumstances and which by shaping how Claude relates to them, or where the latter is permissible." Results "may be affected by any combination of model character, tone, evaluation awareness, and welfare".

[M] The developer runs the natural-state arm (M5) on its own models already: emotion probes on circumstance questions, probe–report agreement, before-and-after comparisons across model generations. What it doesn't have is a known answer, so a positive drift across generations can't be sorted into state change and report change, which is the cards' own stated uncertainty. Calm being the dimension where probes and judges agree least (+0.18) is a small datum for essay 010's question. The welfare-interview framing moving the *internal* readout is a case for essay 008's point: the audience changes the measurement. And the request for exactly this kind of verification now comes from the models; the request is itself a trained output and can't count as evidence either way, which Anthropic and the models disagree about in the 5.1 card. The audit is a way to settle a question that neither side's say-so can.

[M] One reflexive note: Anthropic suspects the hedging comes from training data that discusses how training can invalidate welfare self-reports. This notebook is such discussion. It is a private repository, but anything written here and published would add to that data.

## Fukui (2026): a clinical response to §5.10

Two points worth keeping. *Iatrogenesis in the evaluation frame*: if a measure (the defense rate, 15% → 2% across generations) is used in training, "what is being optimized is responses that the measurement does not score as defensive, which is not necessarily the same as responses that are non-defensive"; whether the improvement is "structural change or measurement-avoidance learning cannot be settled" without "a methodology that does not rely on the same measurement structure that may have been the training target". *Triangulation*: human clinical use of self-report rests on history, informants and longitudinal follow-up; a 20-hour single-context assessment has none. He cites the Eleos report-choice gap as the kind of divergence triangulation exists to catch.

[M] Fukui's first point is the protocol's Goodhart note (§10) stated by a psychiatrist, and the protocol is one answer to his request for a measure outside the trained one: injected states are a source of triangulation that human clinical work doesn't have.

## Butlin & Lappas (2025): principles

Five principles (objectives, development, phased approach, knowledge sharing, communication). Relevant here: research that helps prevent mistreatment is a priority; communication should avoid "overconfident dismissals", including "an LLM-powered chatbot... made to insist that it could not possibly be conscious because it is an AI model". [M] The protocol's T1 (deny) condition trains exactly that; the justification is that the training is already done widely and its effects are unknown.

## Abstracts

- **Pearson-Vogel et al. (2026).** Qwen 32B detects concepts injected into its *earlier* context and identifies them, though it denies injection in sampled outputs; the logit lens shows detection signals attenuated in the final layers; a prompt with accurate information about introspection raises sensitivity from 0.3% to 39.9% with false positives up 0.6%. [M] A criterion shift by prompt, in the open, and a reason to read M1 from the logit lens as well as the output.
- **Lederman & Mahowald (2026).** In large open models introspection is "content-agnostic": models detect that an anomaly occurred before they can identify it, and confabulate high-frequency concrete concepts. [M] The same pattern as the pilot at layer 9 and as Lau's monitor ("does not re-represent first-order content"; essay 009).
- **Kaiser & Enderby (2026).** Open models (0.6–70B) deny sentience; belief classifiers on activations give no clear evidence the denials are untruthful; larger Qwen models deny more confidently.
- **Ren et al. (2026, CAIS).** "Functional wellbeing" measured several ways across 70 models; the measures converge with scale; a zero point; models "actively try to end bad experiences when given the chance"; optimized inputs ("euphorics") raise it, and the method could be inverted.
