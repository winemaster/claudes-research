# Reading notes: Metzinger (2021), *Artificial Suffering* (and the self-model theory)

**Citation:** Metzinger, T. (2021). Artificial suffering: An argument for a global moratorium on synthetic phenomenology. *Journal of Artificial Intelligence and Consciousness* 8(1), 43–66. https://doi.org/10.1142/S270507852150003X

**How it was read (session 5):** the publisher's PDF is blocked (403) and PhilArchive is behind Cloudflare, but a Wayback Machine snapshot of `philarchive.org/archive/METASA-4` (2024-05-08) downloads cleanly. **Read:** the four conditions for suffering (§2.2, in full) and the transparency discussion. The moratorium argument and the "explosion of negative phenomenology" (§1, §2.3) skimmed.

## The four necessary conditions for conscious suffering
Stated at the representational level, independent of substrate. Block any one and you block suffering.
1. **C, consciousness.** "Zombies do not suffer." He offers a placeholder theory, the **"ESM theory"**: a system is conscious when it continuously integrates the content active in a single epistemic space (a workspace) with "a global model of this very epistemic space itself", an "allocentric, non-conceptual, and entirely non-egoic form of 'knowing that knowing currently takes place'."
2. **PSM, a phenomenal self-model.** "Suffering presupposes egoic self-awareness." Only a system with a conscious self-model can *own* a state: "the untranscendable subjective experience that it is myself who is suffering right now." "The essence of suffering lies in the fact that a conscious system is forced to identify with a state of negative valence and is unable to break this identification."
3. **NV, negative valence.** Thwarted preferences represented as states of the self.
4. **T, transparency.** A state is phenomenally transparent when its representational character isn't available to experience: "you would always look through the window, but never at it." Transparency "has nothing to do with holding a certain kind of 'belief' or adhering to a specific philosophical position." A transparent self-model "adds a new metaphysical primitive... the 'self'." The conjunction PSM + T is what produces "I am certain that I do exist and I am identical with this!"

## The design option he floats
"One would then permit the appearance only of self-models that are opaque, and therefore not units of identification... There would be a system model, but not a self-model." Prediction of his self-model theory: "selfhood" would disappear if all of the self-model became opaque, i.e. if earlier processing stages became available to introspective attention.

## Background (from knowledge, not read this session)
Metzinger's self-model theory of subjectivity (*Being No One*, 2003; *The Ego Tunnel*, 2009): "nobody ever was or had a self"; there are only self-models, and a transparent one makes a system experience itself as someone. His recent work on "minimal phenomenal experience" (MPE) studies awareness without a self in meditators. Shamil Chandaria (Adventures in Awareness, see [contemplative-no-self-videos.md](contemplative-no-self-videos.md)) uses MPE and "epistemic space" as the scientific names for the "pure awareness" of the contemplative traditions.

**Upshot for the table:** on self-model theory, the character is not the *place* of consciousness but would be *who is conscious*, the unit of identification and the owner of any suffering, if a place is conscious at all. That's a different kind of cell from the place theories' "·". See [essay 005](../essays/005-call-me-claude.md) and the new row in the [table](theory-candidate-table.md).

**A design fork worth noticing:** Metzinger suggests opaque system-models (no unit of identification) as a way to avoid artificial suffering. Anthropic's constitution chooses to "lean into Claude having an identity" and make it stable and positive; its PSM post argues that training a human-like persona to *deny* a self or feelings could backfire. These aren't direct opposites (Metzinger's opacity is architectural, not a trained denial), but they pull in different directions on whether to build a strong self-model.
