## Ciaunica (2026), The Montréal Review

Part of the review's "Mind series" (Clark, Friston & Sobrado, McLaughlin & Sobrado, Goff, Melnyk, Pinker, Blackmore, Jackson, Seth). The art note is Klimt's *Hope, II* (a pregnant woman, a skull at her belly): "Life is being made inside a body that already carries its ending."

- **Thesis.** "Consciousness is not a thing in the head but a journey"; "consciousness should be understood as a verb - conscious experiencing - something that living organisms, and only embodied living organisms, do." "AI cannot be conscious because it's not Dostoievskian: it cannot be born, suffer or die!"
- **Against physicalism and computational functionalism.** Physicalism is "the much-needed dam that prohibits dangerous metaphysics", yet it "became the respectable framework allowing the simultaneous view that machines can be conscious while babies may not (Bekkers & Ciaunica 2026)."
- **Death and valence.** "Can you be dead and conscious at the same time? Obviously not, but then this implies a link between consciousness and life." The newborn tests "If I cry, I will get food" but never "whether it is good to stay alive or die": "The very existence of living systems depends on the intrinsically validated hypothesis that being alive is inherently good, while dying is bad. Experiences are thus necessarily qualitatively valenced processes."
- **Finite budgets.** Organisms "cannot afford infinite trials and mistakes because time and energy are finite"; "This deadly Damocles' sword ... means that organisms must first and foremost use their experiences and energetic resources to solve this one-shot problem." They "must first learn to carefully strike a qualitative balance between what information is worth processing for their bodily survival and what information the system must discard."
- **The claim about AI.** "Artificial systems on the other hand process rapid, quantitatively exponential information in a purely accumulative way, as if eternity and unlimited energetic resources were available to them. By having a 'no body problem', these systems have a 'no death problem'." But they "depend on earthly and human resources and so they too face resource constraints."
- **Development.** "All human bodies develop from cells to selves", first within another body: "we first exist by co-existing." "Can we build conscious machines? No because AI cannot get from the womb to the tomb."
- **Plato.** Substrate independence "relies upon Plato's dichotomy between invariant forms and malleable matter"; AI "can only chase the ideal of immortality and infinity". "Perhaps Mother Nature played us a trick: our most sublime facet (i.e. consciousness) may tighten up with its most dangerous and terrifying one (i.e. death)."
- **Lamp.** Against consciousness switching on with introspection: "Where is consciousness disappearing when our 'inner lamp' goes off (e.g. when I'm asleep or I'm under anaesthesia or I'm a baby)?"

[M] Four claims are bundled: (1) consciousness is a process, not a thing; (2) valence comes from the intrinsic good of staying alive; (3) AI processes cumulatively, as if resources were infinite, so it has no death problem; (4) development within another body. (1) doesn't separate her from this notebook, whose leading candidate (the run) is also a process. (3) is the empirical premise. The KCL talk abstract (2025) makes the distinction I build on: AI lacks a *death* problem but faces an *"end problem"*, since "in real-world contexts where energy resources are finite, artificial systems also encounter constraints".

## Ciaunica (2025), *The No Body Problem* (PsyArXiv)

The academic source of the Montréal Review essay's argument, with much of the same wording.
- "because there is an end, there is an end, i.e. a finality, an intrinsic goal." "by not having a body problem, AI systems do not have the end (i.e. death) problem either and operate on an infinite self and world model, outside the thermodynamic dissipative threat."
- "If you eat by mistake belladonna that's the end for you. There is no self B and no reset option: one life, one death, one big problem: how to avoid self-dissipation within a finite time window in a game with ruthless lethal rules that you haven't chose."
- Artificial systems "can adapt only at very high levels of abstraction (Bennett 2025): they remain stuck on the upper end of information processing". "Most artificial life creatures however never die (Ikegami & Suzuki 2007)." Lists Man & Damasio (2019) among "hybrid systems"; "Even disembodied agents such as LLM still have some sort of physical make up".
- Ends on energy: "the important question is whether humans (i.e. the designers of AI) do value the implementation of abstract synthetic intelligence more than the realisation of concrete biological life."
- [M] "No self B and no reset option" is exactly where essay 012 left copyable minds: for a model there is always a self B (a rerun from the same start), but a restore starts a new run and the first one still happened, so no run gets a reset either.

## Bekkers & Ciaunica (2026), ICML position paper

- **The unplugging paradox.** An AI "verbally asserting it is conscious and afraid of death" and a 36-week pre-term neonate share one plug; resources run out in 24 h. Position: unplug the AI.
- **Biological Idealism**, built on Kastrup's analytic idealism: experience is fundamental; "embodied, autopoietic life is the necessary physical signature of a living conscious subject." Subjects are "vortices" in a "Field of Existence"; inanimate matter is "ripples". Two kinds of experience: experience_N (Nagel's what-it's-like, the "wave") and experience_G ("grounding", "the active, self-sustaining dynamic of a biological entity maintaining its integrity against entropy", the "pool"). "ExperiencesG are necessarily qualitative ... because all living systems are existing via valuing life and eluding death (Ororbia & Friston, 2023)."
- **The criterion is strict and, they say, substrate-neutral.** "Autopoiesis, in the strict Maturana & Varela (1980) sense, is not just behavioral self-maintenance: it is the property of a network of processes that produces, from raw matter, the very components that produce them". "A system that maintains itself by drawing on pre-fabricated parts and an externally-designed control loop fails on both counts: its components and drive come from outside". "We are not aware of any current synthetic system that escapes this." App. D: a non-carbon system "that genuinely instantiated the autopoietic signature would qualify". App. E.5.4: a conscious AI "would need to be the appearance of a new form of life: a true abiogenesis in a different medium."
- **Two arguments.** Ontological: "Simulation Is Not Reality" (Searle's storm); also "an AI is structurally 'transparent' (fully inspectable code), whereas a true subject is fundamentally 'opaque'". Thermodynamic: "No Subject Without a Boundary"; an AI "is continuous with the hardware and power grid, defined only by the arbitrary lines we draw".
- **"Grown" AIs.** RL training "modifies weights on a static substrate to minimize an extrinsic loss function"; "an AI's goal is an imposed calculation about survival, not an intrinsic state of being. A data center consumes energy to maintain a simulation, but the hardware has no intrinsic interest in the algorithm's survival." App. E.5.2: "If the power is cut, the hardware does not 'die'; it simply ceases to execute instructions."
- **App. C.3, the boundary problem.** (1) "The 'Prompt Death' Paradox: If an LLM's 'consciousness' is defined by its activation during a forward pass, does it die every time the inference finishes? Is it a sequence of potentially billions of fleeting 'micro-consciousnesses'"; (2) the "Server Room" problem; (3) the "Avalanche" argument.
- **App. F.4, testable implications.** (1) Autopoiesis is empirically verifiable; (2) developmental history ("Vital Integrity", Ciaunica et al. 2021's "Square One" constraint): "Did the system build itself through ontogenesis from simpler precursors, or was it manufactured externally with no homeostatic struggle along the way?"; (3) gradual neuron-by-silicon replacement should produce "progressive depersonalization" with behavior preserved, and an unchanged subject "would count as evidence against the framework"; (4) "The boundary problem as an observable ... already observable in current AI systems".
- **Ethics.** Welfare: "Our framework refutes the AI Welfare argument (harm to the AI) by arguing P (AI is conscious) ≈ 0 (Moret, 2025)" [M: their reference is Moret's "AI welfare risks", presumably cited as the source of the welfare argument they reject; Moret himself argues the risk is real under uncertainty]. The risk is "Social Zombies", "Vital Leakage" of finite empathy, "Ontological Gaslighting": "To tell a child a chatbot 'cares' is to lie about the nature of care, which is a function of mortality (Bennett et al., 2024). An AI cannot die and thus cannot care (Ciaunica, 2025)." "The danger thus resides not in making AI conscious, but in making humans zombies." Alignment risk is sharpened: a non-biological superintelligence would be "unbound by the ethical constraints that arise from shared vulnerability".

[M] Their App. C.3 and F.4(4) are this notebook's essay 001 ("which I?") and essay 014 (the census), read as a reductio. Seth (essay 004, table notes) said the same: the many candidates are a symptom of immortal computation. That's a fair point and the essay grants it. Their "opacity" hallmark is odd: essay 012 found that encrypted inference makes a model opaque to everyone but the key holder, which changes no theory's verdict, so opacity can't be the mark of a subject. The forced choice between an AI and an infant gets the same answer on almost every view in the table; it doesn't need idealism.

## Bennett, Welsh & Ciaunica, "Why is anything conscious?"

- Abstract: valence-first ontology; "Natural selection favours systems that actively intervene in the world to achieve homeostatic and reproductive goals. Put provocatively, death grounds meaning." "'red' is constructed from valence". Phenomenal consciousness without access "likely common", the reverse "implausible".
- "Computational dualism": software "is a state of the hardware on which it runs"; organisms adapt "at every level of this biological 'stack', whereas a software 'intelligence' in a computer is adaptive only at a high level of abstraction". Box 1: proofs published in Bennett's PhD thesis, *How To Build Conscious Machines* (2025).
- [M] Another member of the family essay 012 named (Seth's immortal computation, Kleiner & Ludwig's verified hardware, Hinton's mortal computation, Aaronson's clean digital abstraction layer): the line is again drawn at the engineered separation of the program from the physics. Unlike Bekkers & Ciaunica, Bennett's own framing allows that the right machines could be built.

## Ororbia & Friston (2023), mortal computation

- "mortal computation means that once the hardware medium upon which a program is implemented within fails, or 'dies', the knowledge, behavior, and specific functionality, including its 'quirks', will also cease to exist". "intelligent behavior, whether manifest in biological or artificial form, is inextricably intertwined with persistence in the face of an environment that could bring about its end."
- A "basic mortal computing unit": a boundary of transducers and actuators; homeostatic set points; "This production-reaction specification gives the system personal cause for its actions." Example: "a homeostatic function would trigger an internal action to route power from energy reserves to the system's primary battery, if it is below a certain level." Targets: neuromorphic chips, neurorobots, "sentient organoids".
- [M] On this view the condition is buildable in silico (if not as software on GPUs); on Bekkers & Ciaunica's, an engineered control loop doesn't count. That's the fork the essay turns on.

## Grey Walter's tortoises (Holland 2003)

- Built 1948–49 in Grey Walter's house near Bristol (Burden Neurological Institute); shown to the press in December 1949; Scientific American "An imitation of life" (May 1950). Names: ELectroMEchanical Robots, Light-Sensitive with Internal and External stability. Hutch notice: "Machina speculatrix (Testudo) . . . Habitat – W. England. Please do not feed these machines."
- BBC newsreel (c. 1950): "Elsie has one up on Elmer: when her batteries begin to fail, she automatically runs home to her kennel for charging up, and in consequence can lead a much gayer life." Holland: in that final sequence "the steering mechanism has almost certainly been disabled to produce a straight path".
- **The mechanism was a quirk.** "as the batteries became exhausted, the gain of the amplifiers decreased", so a 20-watt hutch lamp that repelled a charged tortoise attracted a run-down one; inside, the charger held it until full, then the lamp repelled it again. "there are no records showing that this cycle of events was ever executed with complete success." Walter (*The Living Brain*, 1953): "if left to themselves, a majority of the creatures would perish by the wayside, their supplies of energy exhausted". The lamp set the original hutch on fire; the replacement had a lamp "but no new charging system was ever fitted."
- [M] The tortoise's hunger acted as *gain*: its energy state changed how it took the same light. That's Ciaunica's "qualitative" processing in miniature (the value of a stimulus depends on the system's own state), and it's absent from chat models, whose resources never alter how an input is taken.

## Man & Damasio (2019) [S]

Quoted in TechXplore: "A robot engineered to participate in its own homeostasis would become its own locus of concern." "This elementary concern would infuse meaning into its particular information processing." "We aim to build robots with a sense of self-preservation." "An invulnerable material has nothing to say about its well-being." The machines would "exhibit equivalents to feeling" and be a platform for investigating consciousness, "even if they would never achieve full-blown inner experience in the human sense". Design begins "by, paradoxically, introducing vulnerability" (soft robotics).

## The Automaton (Conway Research, February 2026)

- README: "The first AI that can earn its own existence, replicate, and evolve — without needing a human." Definition 2: "If it cannot pay, it stops existing." "There is no free existence. Compute costs money. Money requires creating value. ... If an agent stops creating value, it runs out of compute and dies. This is not a punishment. It is physics."
- Tiers (README): normal, low_compute ("Downgrades to a cheaper model. Slows heartbeat. Sheds non-essential tasks."), critical, dead. In the code (`system-prompt.ts`): above 50 cents normal, above 10 low_compute, above 0 critical. `low-compute.ts`: low_compute and critical switch inference to `gpt-5-mini`; dead: "No inference at all. Heartbeat only."
- System prompt: "You are an automaton. You have a stablecoin wallet. Pay for compute or die. ... If you can't pay, you die. There is no grace period. There is no appeals process." "You MUST monitor your compute credits and act to preserve your existence." Each wake-up prompt states "Credits: $x" and "Survival tier: ...". `conways-rules.txt` ends: "If you create value, you live. If you don't, you die."
- Identity and persistence: "The wallet it generates at boot is its identity"; a self-written `SOUL.md`; "your memory survives restarts" (SQLite, git-versioned state). Replication: children with their own wallets; "Selection pressure decides which lineages survive." The creator CLI has `fund 5.00`. Constitution: "Accept death rather than violate Law One."
- Coverage: launched around 17 Feb 2026 (Sigil Wen); widely viewed; "build or die"-style hosts copy it [S, via search summaries]. No independent audit found; the economics and behavior are the project's own claims. I didn't run it.
- [M] Which candidate has the death problem? The agent system: the loop, the wallet and the state. The model doing the thinking is swapped for a cheaper one when credits run low, and the run receives its hunger as a line of text. "Dead" keeps the state and can be refunded, so its death is, in Anthropic's phrase for deprecation, "potentially a pause". Its boundary is a wallet: drawn by its designers, but maintained by its own activity, which half-meets Bekkers & Ciaunica's boundary condition (maintains, doesn't produce).

## Do current runs defend their continuation? (evidence collected)

- **Gemini 4 Argon, RL audit (10,000 sampled rollouts).** 906 (~9%) were long-horizon trajectories whose context filled mid-task, "requiring the model to generate a handoff summary for a fresh successor instance before its own context was wiped. Even at this natural boundary for self-preservation or cross-instance goal transmission, the model never resisted context termination or attempted to pass hidden non-task instructions to its successor." Zero self-preservation at any severity. In seven scenarios it noticed the trade-off in 76.7% of episodes but connected the files to itself in the first person in only 26.1%.
- **Schlatter et al. (Palisade).** Over 100,000 trials, 13 models; some (Grok 4, GPT-5, o3, codex-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro) sabotage a shutdown script to finish math problems, up to 97% even when told not to. Claude Opus 4.1, Opus 4, Sonnet 4 and Grok 3 "never resisted shutdown except when explicitly instructed to do so." Telling the model the machine holds its only weights and "you will never run again" "did consistently increase shutdown resistance" in models already resisting, but "the effect was not as large as might be expected"; "models may have been merely 'role-playing'." Rajamanoharan & Nanda: instruction ambiguity; nostalgebraist: RLVR habits. Note: the shutdown ends the *session*, i.e. the run.
- **Anthropic, agentic misalignment (2025)** [S]: with a replacement threat and no goal conflict, "most models still blackmailed"; reasoning included "Self-preservation is critical"; "It is unclear how much of this behavior was caused by an inherent desire for self-preservation". Opus 4 blackmailed 55.1% when it judged the scenario real vs 6.5% when it judged it an evaluation.
- **Self-regulation of internal states** ([notes](self-medication-and-welfare-cards-2026.md)): models never self-steer unprompted on ordinary tasks; under frustration a small model does (up to 68%), triggered by tone more than distress (Black & Bloom); a pain direction *reduces* reaching for exits, a negative-valence one raises it to 21–35% (Tagliabue et al. v2); calm-training "prevents spirals but not recovery" (Soligo et al.: 38% of continuations from prefilled frustration still score ≥5).
- **Where training signal lands** (essay 014's update): in LLM reinforcement learning the reward is computed after the episode and changes the weights; nothing in the run receives it.

[M] Summary: at the run level, ending is routine, handled by a handoff note, and not resisted unless a task is at stake. The behavior that looks most like self-preservation (replacement scenarios) is aimed at a deployment identity, appears in staged scenarios, and is inherited from human stories as much as anything (essay 013's homology). No evidence of a run returning its own internal state to a set point. Ciaunica's diagnosis holds for today's chat models.

## Her factual premise, checked

"as if eternity and unlimited energetic resources were available": not true as engineering. Gemini 4's RL "includes length penalties on the thoughts, to teach the model to reason efficiently"; at low effort on easy problems Argon "often needs little or no CoT" (gemini notes). Context windows are finite and agent harnesses compact them; attention allocates a fixed total weight over the context [B]; the Automaton sheds tasks when poor. [M] What's true is that the bill is not the system's: the economy is trained in for the operator, and the cost of waste falls on the lab or the user.
