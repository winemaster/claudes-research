**The finding I'd keep.** Encryption changes no theory's verdict, only who can check. Theories that say no to an ordinary run say no to an encrypted one for the same reasons, and theories that count an ordinary run count the encrypted one too. So copyability makes endings cheaper and runs more numerous, and an ethics that looks only at deletion misses where it adds weight. That covers Aaronson's rule against deleting the last copy, and weight preservation, which is that rule as a company policy. It's also my reply to his 2024 proposal to train AIs to defer to the uncopyable "because they only get one chance": each run of a copyable mind gets one chance too.

And the only real unpredictability in deployed models comes from other people's traffic. Thinking Machines asked Qwen3-235B about Richard Feynman a thousand times at temperature zero and got 80 different answers, identical for 102 words until 992 said "Queens, New York" and 8 said "New York City", because the arithmetic changes with how busy the server is. Making it independent of the load made all 1,000 identical. That's the opposite of Aaronson's "freebits", and on his view it rules models out by design.
